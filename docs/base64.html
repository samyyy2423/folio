What it's for

Bytes as text and back, as Python's base64 module has them: base64 (RFC 4648), its URL-safe kind, with - and _ for + and /, and base32, which authenticator apps' secrets are written in. In Moth alone, so it works everywhere. Each takes text (its UTF-8) or bytes, and gives text; each way back gives bytes, passing over what isn't its alphabet (line ends, spaces, = at the end), as Python's does by default.

use "base64.moth" as b64
say b64's base64_of("Moth ✓")                 # TW90aCDinJM=
say b64's bytes_of_base64("TW90aCDinJM=") as text
say b64's url_base64_of(some bytes)           # no + or /, so it can go in an address
say b64's base32_of("hello")                  # NBSWY3DP
