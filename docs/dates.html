What it's for

Dates on the calendar: today's, one read from "2026-11-03", days added and counted between, weekdays, and a date written as a person wants it. In Moth alone, so it works everywhere.

use "dates.moth" as dates
set start to dates's read_date("2026-11-03")
set due to dates's add_days(start, 30)
say dates's format_date(due, "dddd D MMMM YYYY")       # Thursday 3 December 2026
say dates's days_between(start, due)                    # 30
say dates's format_date(dates's now(), "YYYY-MM-DD hh:mm:ss")

A date is a record with its year, month and day; now() is one with its hour, minute and second too. format_date writes YYYY (2026), YY (26), MMMM (November), MMM (Nov), MM (11), M, DD (03), D (3), dddd (Tuesday), ddd (Tue), hh (09, from 0 to 23), h, mm (05, minutes), m, ss and s; anything else is as it is, and anything in [ ] is as it is without the brackets: "D [of] MMMM".
