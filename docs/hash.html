What it's for

Hashes, as Python's hashlib and hmac have them: a text's or bytes' SHA-256, SHA-224, SHA-512, SHA-384, SHA-1 or MD5, as the hex digits sha256sum and every other program give for the same bytes (a text's are its UTF-8); HMAC, a hash with a key, which says a message came from one who knows it; and PBKDF2, a key made from a password slowly, to keep passwords. In Moth alone, so it works everywhere. (SHA-1 and MD5 are broken for keeping secrets: they're here for checking files and talking to what still uses them.)

use "hash.moth" as hash
say hash's sha256("abc")             # ba7816bf8f01cfea414140de5dae2223b00361a396177a9cb410ff61f20015ad
say hash's sha256(read bytes "moth.exe")
say hash's digest("sha512", "abc")   # any of them by its name: md5, sha1, sha224, sha256, sha384, sha512
say hash's sha224("abc")             # and sha384, sha512; sha1 and md5 for what still uses them
say hash's sha384("abc")
say hash's sha512("abc")
say hash's sha1("abc")
say hash's md5("abc")
say hash's hmac("sha256", "key", "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog")
say hash's pbkdf2("sha256", "password", "salt", 1000, 32)   # 32 bytes, as hex
say hash's bytes_of_hex("ff00")      # (2 bytes: 255, 0); hex_of(bytes) back
