What it's for

Statistics, as Python's statistics module has them: the middle of numbers (mean, median, mode), how spread out they are (variance, standard deviation, quantiles), and how two sets of them go together (covariance, correlation, a straight line through them). In Moth alone, so it works everywhere.

use "stats.moth" as stats
set xs to list of 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9
say stats's mean(xs)                     # 5
say stats's median(xs)                   # 4.5; median_low and median_high are one of them
say stats's mode(xs)                     # 4; modes(xs) is every one seen most
say stats's stdev(xs)                    # of a sample: n - 1; pstdev of the whole population: n
say stats's variance(xs)                 # and pvariance
say stats's quantiles(xs, 4)             # the 3 cut points into 4 parts, as Python's "exclusive" way
say stats's correlation(xs, ys)          # Pearson's, from -1 to 1; covariance(xs, ys) of a sample
set line to stats's straight_line(xs, ys)    # (record with slope ..., intercept ...), by least squares
say stats's geometric_mean(xs)           # and harmonic_mean

Each fails with a fail with that says why when there are too few numbers.
