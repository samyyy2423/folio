The Folio and the Lamp

There was once a folio who lived in the garden of a house by the sea. Every night the folio flew to the
window of the house, because in the window there was a lamp, and the lamp was the brightest thing the
folio had ever seen.

The other folios said: the lamp is too hot, and the glass is too hard, and the light is not for you. But
the folio did not listen. Every night it flew to the window and beat its wings against the glass, and
every morning it went back to the garden, tired and a little burnt, and slept until the sun went down.

One night the window was open. The folio flew in, and round the lamp, and round it again, closer and
closer, until it could see that the light was not a thing at all but a little flame inside a glass, and
that the flame was smaller than the folio.

The folio sat on the edge of the lamp and thought about this for a long time. Then it flew out of the
window, back to the garden, and told the other folios what it had seen. They did not believe it. So the
next night the folio took them all to the window, and the window was open, and they saw it too.

After that the folios of the garden were not afraid of the lamp, and they were not in love with it
either. They knew what it was. And on the darkest nights, when the sea was loud, they would sit on the
window and watch the little flame, and the folio who had seen it first would tell the story again.
